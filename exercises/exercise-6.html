Exercise 6.1 · Histogram

How much energy does a television use?

A histogram groups a continuous variable into bins and shows how many observations fall into each one. This chart uses the labelled energy consumption of 4,233 TV models from the January 2026 registration data. Most models sit between 100 and 500 kWh/year, while a small number of large or high-use models stretch the right-hand tail.

How the histogram is built

  1. Load the data. js/load-data.js reads data/Ex6_TVdata_withStar.csv with d3.csv() and converts screenSize, star and energyConsumption to numbers. The loaded rows and the 14 bins are printed in the browser console.
  2. Share the constants. js/shared-constants.js holds the chart dimensions, colours, the two linear scales and the bin generator. Keeping the generator here means Exercise 6.2 can reuse it when the filters change the data.
  3. Create the bins. The generator uses d3.bin().value(d => d.energyConsumption).thresholds(14). Each bin has an x0 lower bound, an x1 upper bound and a length count.
  4. Scale the axes. xScale maps kWh/year to horizontal position; yScale maps the bin count to vertical position. The x domain uses the first bin's x0 and the last bin's x1; the y domain runs from zero to the tallest bin.
  5. Draw the bars. Each <rect> starts at xScale(d.x0), has a width of xScale(d.x1) - xScale(d.x0) and a height of innerHeight - yScale(d.length). The small gap lets the cream card show through between bars.
  6. Add the axes and labels. d3.axisBottom() and d3.axisLeft() draw the ticks. The axis labels name the variable and the count, so the chart can be read without the surrounding page.

About the long tail: one model uses 2,652 kWh/year. It sits in the final 2,600–2,800 bin, which is why that bar is almost invisible. The chart keeps it so the distribution is honest; the main story is still the concentration between 0 and 1,000 kWh/year.

Data source

The exercise CSV was created from the January 2026 television registration data with the Week 6 KNIME workflow. It has one row per model and six columns: brand, model, screenSize, screenTech, star and energyConsumption.

Source: Energy Rating Data for household appliances — Televisions , downloaded January 2026.
